### Purpose and context

The project addresses a dual challenge:

1. Lack of affordable basic hygiene products in underserved communities such as the Hadzabe, Iraqw, Barbaig and Maasai.
2. The environmental burden of discarded agricultural waste.

ASE runs a strong pilot model in partnership with local governments and churches. It has trained 3,000 women and youth and produces 2,500 soap bars a month, working from a temporary wooden shed. The main problem is the lack of a permanent, scalable production center and efficient digital infrastructure. Both are needed to reach the goal of 5,000 soap bars a month and to make the work sustainable in the long term. ASE is asking EWB-DK for technical expertise and funding to establish the permanent infrastructure and implement the Digital Circular Community Development (DCCD) model.

### Consequences if the project is not approved

If EWB Denmark does not approve the project, ASE will keep operating from the temporary wooden shed, which limits growth and impact. Production will stay small-scale, inconsistent and vulnerable to weather, so the women and youth ASE supports will have fewer ways to earn an income. Without proper equipment (molds, mixers, cutters, computers for record keeping and printers), ASE cannot deliver quality training, and many groups will not be able to start their own micro-enterprises.

Without a permanent production and training site, ASE cannot expand its programs to more underserved women and youth. The community would also miss out on sustainable, circular-economy solutions such as soap-making, waste-to-value products and clean-energy briquettes. The momentum built with local partners, including the land provided by the church, may slow down, and many potential beneficiaries would stay trapped in poverty because they lack practical skills and economic opportunities.

In short, without this support ASE will be much slower to scale its impact, strengthen community resilience and create long-term, sustainable livelihoods for women and youth in the region.

## Details

### Objectives

The overall objective is to move ASE from a successful community pilot in a wooden shed to a resilient, digitally enabled social enterprise housed in a permanent center. The success criteria are:

1. Construct a permanent Production & Training Center to replace the wooden shed.
2. Procure enough equipment (molds, soap cutter, mixer) for stable production of 5,000–10,000 soap bars a month, and scale up skincare cream production.
3. Establish a robust digital system for record keeping and financial management that links mobile money such as M-PESA with VICOBA (Village Community Banks), for transparent and efficient financial inclusion.

### Expected deliverables

1. A permanent Production and Training Center.
2. New production equipment (molds, mixer, soap cutter) capable of 5,000 soap bars a month.
3. Training materials and modules for women and youth covering soap-making, waste-to-value processing, entrepreneurship, hygiene practices and clean-energy solutions.
4. A working community enterprise model in which beneficiaries earn income through production, sales or their own micro-businesses.
5. A digital workspace with computers and printers for training, administration, product labeling and digital literacy.
6. Better documentation and record keeping through the new digital tools, supporting reporting, accountability and impact tracking.
7. Stronger partnerships with local institutions, including churches, youth groups and women's associations, for long-term sustainability.

### Out of scope

1. Sourcing raw materials other than the main agricultural byproducts (sunflower sludge, beeswax).
2. Buying land. The land for the center is already secured.
3. Developing entirely new product lines beyond optimizing the current soap and cream production.
4. Long-term operational funding, salaries or recurring costs beyond the initial establishment and setup phase.

### Dependencies and requirements

1. A clear land title or lease for the permanent center location.
2. Reliable local electricity and water for the new center.
3. Regulatory and local government compliance: construction approval where required, and adherence to local by-laws, safety guidelines and environmental standards.

## Preliminary timeline

**Month 1 – Project kick-off and preparations**
- Formal agreement and communication with EWB Denmark.
- Site verification and measurements.
- Initial planning meetings with engineers, EWB Denmark volunteers and local contractors.
- Procurement planning for construction materials and tools.

**Month 2 – Construction begins**
- Ground preparation and foundation work.
- Structural construction of the permanent multipurpose building.
- Weekly monitoring with support from EWB Denmark volunteers.

**Month 3 – Construction completed**
- Roofing, wall finishing, and installation of windows and doors.
- Electrical and plumbing setup.
- Internal finishing (floors, shelves, storage spaces).

**Month 4 – Equipment procurement and installation**
- Purchase and installation of soap molds, mixers, cutters and briquette tools.
- Purchase of computers, printers and basic office furniture.
- Testing and calibration of production equipment.

**Month 5 – Training materials**
- Preparation of training modules on soap-making, waste-to-value, hygiene and entrepreneurship.
- Joint review by the ASE team and EWB Denmark volunteers.
- Recruitment of women and youth groups for training.

**Month 6 – Training and operational launch**
- First round of training sessions for the selected groups.
- Full production of soap, briquettes and skincare products begins.
- Monitoring and record-keeping system set up with the new digital tools.
- Community awareness event to introduce the new center.

## Proposed project organization

1. **Competences in the EWB project group:** waste management, information and communication technology (ICT), water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), and civil engineering/construction.
2. **Local partner and community competences (steering group):**
   - ASE founder/director (overall vision and community lead)
   - Finance/VICOBA liaison
   - Production/training manager (technical knowledge of soap production and center management)
   - Civil engineer/construction specialist

## Outstanding issues

A detailed feasibility study is needed. It must cover three key areas:

1. **Engineering and construction:** finalizing the site plan and design for the permanent center.
2. **Technical integration:** assessing whether ICT tools can be used to collect, store and report data to funders for project monitoring and evaluation.
3. **Supply chain:** formalizing long-term MOUs with farmers to secure a steady supply of waste materials.

The study should involve local ICT experts, civil engineers and ASE leadership.

## Additional notes

1. ASE has strong community trust and existing training programs. This project will scale that impact and give the community a lasting asset. ASE is open to working with EWB-DK on design, monitoring and capacity building.
2. **Sustainability:** the project promotes circular-economy practices by turning agricultural and household waste into value-added products such as soap and biomass briquettes.
3. **Gender and youth inclusion:** the project prioritizes equal participation of women and youth, aiming to reduce inequality and create long-term economic opportunities.

## Risks

| Risk | Impact | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Construction delays due to weather or contractor issues | High | Medium | Engage reliable local contractors, schedule work in the dry season, and keep a buffer in the timeline |
| Production tools or digital equipment unavailable | Medium | Medium | Identify several suppliers, order early, and include alternative equipment options |
| Not enough technical support from volunteers | Medium | Low | Schedule regular virtual support sessions, provide clear guidance documents, and keep communication open |
| Maintenance problems or equipment breakdown | Medium | Medium | Train staff in equipment use and maintenance, and set aside a small contingency budget for repairs |
| Regulatory or permit delays | High | Low | Engage local authorities early, comply with by-laws, and document approvals |
